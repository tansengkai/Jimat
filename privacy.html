Privacy Policy

Jimat · Android application · Last updated 24 October 2026

This policy explains how Jimat (“the app”) accesses, uses, stores and shares data — including Google user data. Jimat is a private, personally developed Android application used by its author and his household. It is not a public service and has no sign-up.

1. Summary

  • All budget data lives on your own device.
  • Google access is optional and used only for backup to your own Google Drive.
  • No data is sent to any Jimat-operated server. There is no such server.
  • No advertising, no analytics, no tracking, no data sales.

2. What data the app handles

2.1 Data you enter

Amounts, categories, dates, notes and budget targets that you type into the app. This information is stored in the app's private storage on your device.

2.2 Google user data (only if you choose to sign in)

  • Basic Google account identifier — your email address and account ID, used to show which Google account is connected for backup.
  • Google Drive app-specific files — the backup file the app itself creates. Access is requested with the https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file scope, which limits the app to files it created. The app cannot see, read or modify any other file in your Drive.

Jimat does not request access to Gmail, Contacts, Calendar, Photos, Location, or any other Google product or scope.

Scope note: drive.file is a non-sensitive, per-file scope. It grants access only to files the app creates and to files you explicitly pick with the Google file picker.

3. How the data is used

  • To record, display and summarise your own spending and savings inside the app.
  • To upload a backup copy of your data to your own Google Drive when you tap backup.
  • To download that backup back into the app when you tap restore.
  • To keep you signed in between sessions, so you do not have to authenticate every time.

Data is used solely to provide these features. It is never used for advertising, profiling, scoring, or any secondary purpose, and it is never used to train machine-learning models.

4. How the data is stored

  • On your device: in the app's private, sandboxed storage, readable only by the app under Android's app-isolation model.
  • OAuth tokens: stored locally on the device. Access tokens are short-lived; refresh tokens are kept in the app's private storage.
  • Backups: stored in your own Google Drive, under your own Google account and subject to Google's terms and security. Google's handling of data in your Drive is described in the Google Privacy Policy.
  • No cloud database: the developer operates no server and holds no copy of your data.

5. How the data is shared

The app does not sell, rent, trade or transfer your data to any third party. There are no advertising SDKs, no analytics SDKs, and no crash reporters that transmit personal data.

The only outbound transfer of your data is the one you initiate: uploading a backup to your own Google Drive through Google's API. That transfer is between your device and Google, not the developer.

6. Data retention and deletion

  • In the app: data remains until you delete individual records, clear the app's storage, or uninstall the app. Uninstalling removes all local data.
  • In Google Drive: backups remain in your Drive until you delete them yourself. You can remove them from the Drive app or at drive.google.com.
  • Revoking access: you can revoke the app's Google access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Revoking immediately invalidates the stored tokens; the app can no longer read or write your Drive.
  • Signing out: signing out inside the app deletes the locally stored tokens.

7. Security

  • Authentication uses Google OAuth 2.0 with PKCE; credentials are never handled by the app directly.
  • All communication with Google APIs uses HTTPS/TLS.
  • Local data is protected by Android's app sandbox and, where enabled, device-level encryption and screen lock.
  • Tokens are not transmitted anywhere except to Google's own token endpoint.

No system is perfectly secure. If you lose your device, consider revoking the app's Google access from another device.

8. Google API Services User Data Policy — Limited Use

Jimat's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • Google user data is used only to provide the user-facing features described in this policy.
  • Google user data is not transferred to third parties except as necessary to provide those features, to comply with applicable law, or as part of a merger/acquisition with notice.
  • Google user data is not used for serving advertisements, including personalised or interest-based advertising.
  • No human reads Google user data, except the user whose data it is, or where required for security purposes, to comply with law, or with the user's explicit consent.

9. Children

Jimat is not directed at children under 13 (or the applicable age in your jurisdiction), is not offered to the public, and does not knowingly collect data from children.

10. International users

The app itself does not transmit data across borders. Data stored in Google Drive is handled by Google under its own terms and international data transfer mechanisms.

11. Changes to this policy

Material changes will be published on this page with an updated revision date. Continued use of the app after a change constitutes acceptance of the revised policy.
